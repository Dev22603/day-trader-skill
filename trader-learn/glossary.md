## A business in numbers

- **revenue** (sales, turnover, top line): All the money a business takes in from selling, before any cost is taken off. Lesson A1.
- **cost of goods sold** (COGS, cost of sales): What it cost to make or buy the things that were sold. Lesson A2.
- **gross profit** (gross): Revenue minus the cost of goods sold: what is left before rent, salaries, interest and tax. Lesson A2.
- **operating expenses** (expenses, overheads, opex): The costs of running a business that are not tied to one sale, such as rent and salaries. Lesson A3.
- **operating profit** (EBIT): Gross profit minus operating expenses. Lesson A3.
- **net profit** (net, net income, bottom line, PAT, profit after tax): What is left after every cost, interest and tax: the number the owners keep. Lesson A3.
- **gross and net** (gross vs net, net vs gross): Gross is before costs are taken off, net is after. True for a salary, a business and a trade. Lesson A3.
- **margin** (profit margin, net margin, gross margin): A profit divided by revenue, as a percent. Not the same as trading margin, which is borrowed money. Lesson A4.
- **unrealised profit** (paper profit, unrealized, realised profit, realized): Profit on a trade that is still open; it becomes realised, and yours, only when the trade is closed. Lesson A5.

## Companies and shares

- **share** (stock, equity, scrip): One equal piece of a company; owning it makes you a part-owner. Lesson B1.
- **shareholder** (stockholder): Someone who owns shares of a company. Lesson B1.
- **market price** (price, CMP, current market price): What one share last traded at. Lesson B2.
- **market cap** (market capitalisation, market capitalization, mcap): Share price times the number of shares: what the whole company is valued at. Lesson B2.
- **large cap** (mid cap, small cap, blue chip): Companies grouped by size; large caps are the biggest and usually the steadiest. Lesson B2.
- **dividend** (ex-date, ex-dividend): Part of a company's profit paid in cash to shareholders, so much per share. The price drops by about that much on the ex-date. Lesson B3.
- **split** (stock split, bonus, bonus issue, bonus shares): The company gives you more shares and the price falls in proportion: you hold the same value. Lesson B3.
- **IPO** (initial public offering, listing): A company selling its shares to the public for the first time; afterwards they trade on the exchange. Lesson B4.

## The market

- **stock exchange** (exchange, NSE, BSE, National Stock Exchange, Bombay Stock Exchange): The marketplace where shares are bought and sold. India's two main ones are the NSE and the BSE. Lesson C1.
- **index** (Nifty, Nifty 50, Sensex, benchmark): One number that tracks a basket of shares. The Nifty 50 follows 50 large NSE companies, the Sensex 30 on the BSE. Lesson C1.
- **broker** (Zerodha, Kite, stockbroker): The firm that sends your orders to the exchange. Kite is Zerodha's trading app. Lesson C2.
- **demat account** (demat, depository, CDSL, NSDL): The account where your shares are kept in electronic form. Lesson C2.
- **trading account**: The account you place buy and sell orders from. Lesson C2.
- **SEBI** (regulator, Securities and Exchange Board of India): The regulator that makes the rules for exchanges, brokers and listed companies. Lesson C2.
- **trading hours** (market hours, session, pre-open, closing session): The NSE trades shares from 9:15 am to 3:30 pm on weekdays that are not holidays. Lesson C3.
- **settlement** (T+1, T plus 1): The exchange of money and shares after a trade. In India it happens one trading day later. Lesson C3.
- **delivery** (CNC, cash and carry): Buying shares to keep for more than a day, paying the full price. Zerodha's product type is CNC. Lesson C4.
- **intraday** (MIS, day trade, day trading, margin intraday square off): Buying and selling the same share within one day. Zerodha's product type is MIS. Lesson C4.
- **square off** (auto square-off, square-off, squaring off, flatten, flat): Closing an open position. Zerodha closes open intraday positions itself at 3:20 pm and charges for it. Lesson C4.

## Prices and orders

- **bid** (bid price): The highest price a buyer is offering right now; the price you get if you sell at once. Lesson D1.
- **ask** (offer, ask price): The lowest price a seller is asking right now; the price you pay if you buy at once. Lesson D1.
- **spread** (bid-ask spread): The gap between the bid and the ask: a hidden cost of every trade. Lesson D1.
- **LTP** (last traded price, last price): The price of the most recent trade. Lesson D1.
- **volume**: The number of shares traded in a period. Lesson D2.
- **liquidity** (liquid, illiquid): How easily you can buy or sell without moving the price. Liquid shares have many buyers and sellers. Lesson D2.
- **market order**: An order to buy or sell at once at the best price available: sure to trade, not sure of the price. Lesson D3.
- **limit order** (limit, limit price): An order that names your price: buy at this or lower, sell at this or higher. It may never fill. Lesson D3.
- **slippage**: The gap between the price you expected and the price you got. Lesson D3.
- **stop-loss** (stop, stop loss, SL, SL-M, stop-loss order, stop order): The price where you accept you were wrong and get out; also the waiting order that sells when the price reaches its trigger. Lesson D4.
- **trigger price** (trigger): The price at which a waiting stop-loss order becomes active. Lesson D4.
- **candle** (candlestick, OHLC, bar): Four prices for a period: open, high, low and close. Lesson D5.
- **tick size** (tick): The smallest step a price can move. An order at a price off the tick is refused. Lesson D5.
- **circuit limit** (circuit, upper circuit, lower circuit, price band): The most a share may rise or fall in one day. At the limit, trading can freeze. Lesson D5.

## Reading a company

- **EPS** (earnings per share): Net profit divided by the number of shares. Lesson E1.
- **P/E** (PE, PE ratio, price to earnings, price-earnings): Share price divided by EPS: how many rupees investors pay for each rupee of yearly profit. Lesson E1.
- **results** (earnings, quarterly results, results season, earnings season): A company's report of its revenue and profit, published every three months. Lesson E2.
- **quarter** (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, financial year, FY): Three months. India's financial year runs from April to March, in four quarters. Lesson E2.

## The economy

- **inflation** (CPI, consumer price index): Prices rising over time, so the same money buys less. Lesson F1.
- **RBI** (Reserve Bank of India, central bank): India's central bank; it sets the main interest rate. Lesson F2.
- **repo rate** (interest rate, policy rate): The rate at which the RBI lends to banks. When it rises, borrowing costs more and shares usually fall. Lesson F2.
- **GDP** (gross domestic product, growth, recession): The value of everything a country produces in a year. A recession is when it shrinks. Lesson F3.
- **Union Budget** (budget): The government's yearly plan for taxes and spending, presented on 1 February. Lesson F3.
- **exchange rate** (rupee, dollar, USD INR, forex): The price of one currency in another, such as how many rupees one US dollar costs. Lesson F4.
- **FII** (FPI, DII, foreign institutional investors, domestic institutional investors): Big institutions, foreign (FII) and Indian (DII), whose buying and selling moves the market. Lesson F4.

## Trading basics

- **investing** (investor, long term): Buying part of a business and holding for years. Lesson G1.
- **long** (long trade, going long): Buying first, to gain if the price rises. This pack is long only. Lesson G1.
- **short** (short selling, shorting, going short): Selling first, to gain if the price falls. Off in this pack. Lesson G1.
- **trend** (uptrend, downtrend, sideways): The general direction of a price over time. Lesson G2.
- **support**: A price level where falling prices have stopped before. Lesson G2.
- **resistance**: A price level where rising prices have stopped before. Lesson G2.
- **breakout** (false breakout): The price moving above resistance, or below support, and staying there. Many fail. Lesson G3.
- **opening range** (ORB, opening-range breakout, opening range breakout): The high and low of the first minutes of the day (here 9:15 to 9:30); a breakout is a candle closing above that high. Lesson G3.

## Risk

- **entry** (entry price): The price you buy at. Lesson H1.
- **target** (take-profit, take profit, profit target): The price where you take your profit. Lesson H1.
- **risk a share** (risk per share): Entry price minus stop price: what you lose on each share if the stop is hit. Lesson H1.
- **R** (1R, R multiple, 2R): The amount you risk on a trade, used as a unit: +2R is twice what you risked, -1R is losing it. Lesson H2.
- **reward to risk** (reward:risk, risk reward, R:R, RR, risk-reward ratio): Distance to the target divided by distance to the stop. Lesson H2.
- **position size** (sizing, quantity, how many shares): The number of shares to buy: max loss per trade divided by risk a share. Lesson H3.
- **max loss per trade** (max loss, daily loss limit, max daily loss): The most you accept to lose on one trade; the daily limit is the most in one day. Lesson H3.
- **win rate** (strike rate, hit rate): The share of your trades that win. Lesson H4.
- **expectancy** (edge, average result): The average result of a trade: win rate times average win, minus loss rate times average loss. Lesson H4.
- **leverage** (leveraged, gearing): Trading with money borrowed from the broker. It multiplies gains and losses; you can lose more than you put in. Lesson H5.
- **futures and options** (F&O, FNO, derivatives, derivative): Contracts with leverage built in. About 9 in 10 individual traders lose money in them. Lesson H5.

## Futures, options and borrowed money

- **future** (futures, futures contract): A contract to buy or sell a share or index at a fixed price on a set date, with only a margin put down. The loss is on the whole contract. Lesson K1.
- **lot** (lot size, contract size): The fixed number of shares in one futures or options contract, set by the exchange. It changes: read it in Kite. Lesson K1.
- **mark to market** (MTM, M2M, marked to market): Each day's gain or loss on a futures position being added to or taken from your account. Lesson K1.
- **option** (options, call, put, call option, put option): The right, not the duty, to buy (a call) or sell (a put) at a fixed price until a set date. Kite marks calls CE and puts PE. Lesson K2.
- **premium** (option premium, option price): The price of an option. The buyer pays it and cannot lose more than it. Lesson K2.
- **strike** (strike price): The fixed price at which an option lets its buyer buy or sell. Lesson K2.
- **option buyer** (buying options, option buying, long option): The side that pays the premium. The loss is limited to it, but time decay works against the buyer. Lesson K3.
- **option seller** (option writer, writing options, option selling, selling options, short option): The side that receives the premium. The gain is limited to it; the loss is not. Lesson K3.
- **time decay** (theta, decay): The value an option loses each day just because expiry is nearer. Lesson K3.
- **trading margin** (margin money, margin blocked, margin requirement): The money the broker blocks as security for a position. Not the same as a profit margin. Lesson K4.
- **margin call** (margin shortfall): The broker asking for more money because losses have used up the margin; if you cannot pay, it closes the position. Lesson K4.
- **MTF** (margin trading facility, buying on margin, margin funding): Buying shares for delivery with part of the price lent by the broker, which charges interest every day. Lesson K4.
- **pledging** (pledge, collateral, collateral margin, pledged shares): Giving your own shares to the broker as security in return for margin. The broker can sell them if you end up owing it money. Lesson K4.
- **haircut**: The cut taken off the value of pledged shares when working out the margin they give. Lesson K4.
- **expiry** (expiry date, expiry day, expiration): The date a future or an option ends. Lesson K5.
- **in the money** (ITM, out of the money, OTM, at the money, ATM): An option is in the money when using it would pay; out of the money when it would not, and then it expires worthless. Lesson K5.
- **physical settlement** (physical delivery, delivery at expiry, compulsory delivery): Stock futures and in-the-money stock options end with the whole lot of shares being paid for or handed over, not with cash. Lesson K5.
- **F&O ban** (ban period, ban list): A share whose futures and options positions have reached the exchange's limit: no new positions are allowed until they come down. Lesson K5.

## Costs and tax

- **brokerage**: The broker's fee for a trade. Zerodha intraday: 0.03% of the order or Rs 20, whichever is lower, each way. Lesson I1.
- **STT** (securities transaction tax): A government tax on share trades: 0.025% on the sell side of an intraday trade. Lesson I1.
- **GST** (goods and services tax): An 18% tax on the brokerage and the exchange and SEBI charges. Lesson I1.
- **stamp duty** (exchange transaction charge, transaction charges, SEBI charges, turnover fee): Small government and exchange charges on each trade. Lesson I1.
- **charges** (costs, trading costs, fees): Everything a trade costs: brokerage plus taxes and fees, about 0.1% of the trade for a buy and a sell. Lesson I1.
- **gross result** (gross P&L, before costs): What the price move gave you, before charges. Lesson I2.
- **net result** (net P&L, after costs, P&L, PnL, profit and loss): The gross result minus all charges: what actually reaches your account. Lesson I2.
- **speculative business income** (tax on intraday, intraday tax, ITR-3): How India taxes intraday profit: as business income at your slab rate, not as capital gains. Lesson I3.
